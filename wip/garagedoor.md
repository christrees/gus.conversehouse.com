

If your garage door spring is completely hidden inside a metal pipe or tube, you have a proprietary system known as a Wayne Dalton TorqueMaster Counterbalance System. [1, 2] 
Unlike standard garage doors where you can clearly see a large, exposed coil spring wrapped around a bar, the TorqueMaster design purposefully hances safety and aesthetics by fully enclosing the spring inside the steel tube. [1, 3, 4] 
## How to Confirm It’s Broken
Because the spring is concealed, you won't see the typical 2-inch visual gap that exposes a standard broken spring. Instead, look for these classic TorqueMaster failure signs: [4] 

* 
* Extreme Weight: If you pull the red emergency release cord, the door will feel almost impossible to lift manually. [5] 
* The Opener Stalls: The electric motor will struggle, lift the door just a few inches, and then click or shut off because it cannot handle the unassisted weight. [4] 
* Slack Lift Cables: The steel cables running down the left and right sides of your door will look visibly loose, slack, or may have completely unspooled from their round plastic drums. [4] 
* 

Because the spring is completely contained inside the tube, it cannot dangerously whip outward if it snaps during service. For this reason, Wayne Dalton originally marketed this system as more "homeowner friendly" for DIY installations compared to high-tension traditional torsion springs. [6, 7] 
However, replacing these internal springs comes with unique challenges:

   1. More Moving Parts: The system uses small plastic gears, winding assemblies, and proprietary internal plastic sleeves that must be kept intact. [6, 8] 
   2. No "Universal" Parts: You cannot buy a generic spring off the shelf. You must order a replacement explicitly built for your exact door model, height, and total door balance weight (often found on a sticker on the door or estimated using a scale). [2, 7, 9] 
   3. Winding Differences: Instead of using dangerous steel winding bars, TorqueMaster systems are typically tensioned or unwound using a standard socket wrench or ratchet on a small end gear. [5, 8] 

If you plan to fix it yourself, you will need to take down the entire pipe assembly, slide the broken internal components out, and order a matching TorqueMaster replacement kit. Many homeowners choose this moment to hire a professional to convert the whole system over to a standard, universally available external torsion spring setup for easier future maintenance.
